How consistently do experts rate differences between pianists?

Very differently per scale. Pedal, pace and timing differences are rated consistently (noise ceiling 0.62–0.83); brightness, valence, energy and loudness barely (0.14–0.23). With about 10 raters per segment, a single segment's score is uncertain by about ±0.75 points.

What do the descriptors track in the playing?

Tempo, timing and sustain pedaling. Pace reaches R² = 0.43 from MIDI features, 62% of what the raters' consistency allows. Interpretation, valence and balance stay below 20% of their ceiling.

What do they miss?

Pedal-change frequency and recorded soft-pedal use recur across passages (moderate performer consistency, ICC 0.30–0.34), yet the 19 ratings do not predict them (R² ≈ 0). The CC67 check below keeps the soft-pedal pattern after excluding event-free segments. Whether listeners can hear these habits is open: the experts heard a virtual-piano rendering, and a renderer can drop the soft pedal entirely.

Can a shorter questionnaire preserve the information?

Selecting four scales on the training ratings retains 94.6% [44.9%, 100.3%] of the held-out gain of the all-19-scale model when a complete piece group is left out. The mean of 200 random four-scale forms retains 55.4%. Holding out pianist groups gives 94.5% [47.3%, 99.0%].

Selection, rater calibration and prediction coefficients use training data only. The 20 piece groups keep overlapping 4-, 8- and 16-bar excerpts together; these groups come from four repertoire categories. The earlier 91% result was an in-sample, exploratory estimate for a fixed four-scale form. This validates a selection procedure within PercePiano, not those exact four items on a new dataset. Gain is the reduction in reconstruction error after correcting shared rating noise. Absolute gain is 65.7% of the estimated true-score variance, compared with 69.5% for all 19 scales. The two D960 movement groups contribute 92.2% of the all-19 gain; 204 scored test segments include raters without a training offset. Intervals resample piece groups with fitted models fixed; they omit training and calibration uncertainty.

Does the soft-pedal finding depend on missing controller records?

A MIDI file without CC67 messages does not establish whether the pianist avoided the soft pedal or whether it was not recorded. A sensitivity check keeps recorded zero-valued messages and compares five predefined recording-evidence subsets. 26 of 1,073 segments (2.4%) have no CC67 event; all 20 pianists have an event in at least one segment. Restricting to recorded-event segments gives performer ICC +0.343 [+0.304, +0.408] and held-out ridge R² -0.009 [-0.016, -0.003]; the stable-signature, weak-prediction pattern remains on that subset.
